# Two-stage referral rewards

An invitation is bound at registration to an existing enabled account. Registration increments the invitation count and can pay a small, explicitly configured inviter advance. The larger tail requires the first verified cash payment to qualify. There is no automatic invitee referral gift. General `QuotaForNewUser` gifts are unchanged. Existing balances and historical affiliate totals are preserved; old signup rewards are not retroactively attributed or clawed back.

## Policy and rollout

Configure **Billing → Quota Settings**:

- `ReferralRegistrationRewardQuota`: the inviter's registration advance in ledger quota units. Default **0 (off)**. Enable a small amount deliberately after checking the abuse controls. Existing invitations do not receive a new advance.
- `QuotaForInviter`: the additional first-payment tail, not the combined total. Its existing configured value is retained; 0 disables new tails where no earlier advance policy was snapshotted.
- `ReferralMinTopUpAmounts`: explicit minimum **real amounts actually paid**, by provider settlement currency. Defaults are `{"USD":"10","CNY":"70"}`. A first payment **equal to or greater than** the configured amount qualifies. The currency amounts are independent limits, not an asserted exchange rate. Unlisted currencies cannot earn the tail. Gifts, discounts, affiliate transfers and platform credit amounts cannot increase the verified cash payment.
- `ReferralMinTopUpQuota`: retained optional additional floor on credited platform quota; 0 disables this extra condition. It never replaces the real-money floor.
- `ReferralMaxRewardQuota`: cap on the **combined advance and tail**, with 0 meaning no additional cap.
- `ReferralPenaltyPercent` / `ReferralMaxPenaltyQuota`: existing abuse-penalty controls, default 20 percent and no additional cap. A penalty still requires explicit moderation approval.

Quota values must be non-negative JavaScript-safe integers. Cash floors must be positive, have at most six decimal places and use unique uppercase three-letter currency keys (maximum 32). Invalid published cash policy blocks tail awards, not the payer's ordinary credit. Payment compliance confirmation is required for both stages. `QuotaForInvitee` remains a retired compatibility option with no reward effect.

When an advance is actually paid, the tail amount, floors, combined cap and penalty settings are snapshotted with that reward. Editing the public policy does not rewrite the promised tail. Current account eligibility and compliance are still rechecked at settlement. Invitations with no paid advance use the current policy at the first real payment.

Example configuration: advance 50,000 ledger units, tail 450,000 units and USD minimum 10. Registration pays 50,000; a first payment of USD 10 pays another 450,000. A first payment of USD 9.99 pays no tail, and a later USD 20 payment cannot recover it. Units are the deployment's ledger units; this example does not assert a fixed exchange rate or change live settings.

The first verified cash top-up sets a permanent eligibility marker even if rewards are disabled, the threshold is not met, the inviter is ineligible, or the account already has a historical successful cash top-up. Do not reset that marker on refund or appeal. No retroactive catch-up is performed. Manual order completion is explicitly excluded from referral eligibility and does not consume the first real top-up. Historical manual completions cannot reliably be distinguished from older paid orders, so legacy successful cash rows conservatively consume eligibility.

Only signed settlement through `CompleteExternalTopUp` grants rewards: Stripe, Creem, Waffo, Waffo Pancake, and CNY ePay cash payments. LinuxDO/community credits, wallet transfers, gifts, admin adjustments, manual completions, and subscriptions do not qualify. The standard payment-evidence and quota-capacity validation remains in force. There is one reward row per invitee and one per top-up, enforced in the database. The order ID is nullable until the first verified payment. The advance and tail have separate append-only ledger entries; repeat callbacks cannot repeat either award. Payment credit, first-payment marker, reward credit and ledger entry commit or roll back together.

## Moderation and refunds

Ordinary **Disable/Enable** does not touch rewards. Use **Ban for abuse** for a confirmed abuse or bulk-registration decision; evidence is required. The corresponding reward is revoked. The extra penalty checkbox defaults off and requires explicit administrator confirmation of inviter involvement. Same-IP inference alone is not a supported automatic penalty trigger. Policy is snapshotted when a reward is earned; later setting changes do not alter that grant's penalty.

`POST /api/user/manage` accepts `action=ban_abuse`, `reason=abuse|bulk_registration`, `evidence` (1–1000 bytes), `penalize_inviter` and a stable `request_id`. Reuse the exact payload and request ID on retry; changing it is rejected. The acting administrator is revalidated against persisted status/role and must outrank the target. No reward means no affiliate financial deduction. Repeated bans never deduct the same grant again.

Use **Overturn abuse ban** (`action=restore_referral`, `reason=mistaken_ban`, evidence, a new request ID, no penalty flag) to enable the account and append reversal entries. Replaying a previously processed ban after an appeal cannot disable the account or charge again. An ordinary re-enable does not silently restore rewards. A subsequent new confirmed ban may revoke a restored award once again, with a new revision.

Cumulative full refund of the original top-up revokes both the earned advance and tail without a new abuse penalty. When the first payment failed the tail floor, a full refund still revokes any linked advance. Partial refunds retain it until the original paid amount is fully refunded. If a full refund follows a ban, an appeal may reverse the penalty but cannot restore the invalid reward. Refund provider verification and existing payer-wallet refund constraints are unchanged.

## Accounting, privacy and operations

`users.aff_quota` is a signed reward-only balance. Negative values are debt; future rewards repay it before transfer becomes possible. Purchased `users.quota` is never reduced by referral moderation, even if a former reward was already transferred or spent. `aff_history` remains lifetime gross earned credit, not net earnings. The self API exposes non-negative `aff_quota` plus `aff_debt`. The reward history API `GET /api/user/self/aff/rewards?before=<cursor>` returns at most 50 entries, available balance, debt and current public policy. Identity comes only from authentication; no invitee emails, payment IDs or private evidence are returned. Entries show registration advance, first-payment tail, clawback, penalty and appeal reversal separately. The assistant returns available affiliate credit, affiliate debt, wallet credit and lifetime gross credit as separate fields. Its deprecated `pending_reward_*` field is only an alias for already-earned available affiliate credit; it is not a count or value of unpaid invitations. Neither a paid-invitee count nor a missing-wallet balance can be inferred by dividing lifetime earnings by the current reward.

Main and fast migrations create `referral_rewards`, `referral_ledger_entries`, `referral_moderation_events` and add the first-payment/manual-exclusion columns. Do not drop them on application rollback. Roll all registration, settlement and moderation writers to this version together. Complete schema preparation (including a nullable reward order ID) before enabling registration advances; older settlement writers do not understand staged rewards. Do not roll back to a one-stage writer while advance rows exist. A separate credit-denomination migration must explicitly handle pending advances and their policy snapshots before it can be used with this version. Keep a database backup and run the provided payment/concurrency/moderation/refund tests before enabling the policy. No production balances or options are modified by tests.
